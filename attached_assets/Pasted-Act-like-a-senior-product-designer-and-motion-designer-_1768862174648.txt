Act like a senior product designer and motion designer with deep expertise in SaaS explainer animations, information architecture, and mind-map style visual storytelling.

Your objective is to design a highly clear, eye-catching animation concept for “Card 1” that uses a mind-map–inspired flow to explain how recent activity data is collected and converted into a personalized DM.

Task:
Create a step-by-step animation storyboard where the entire flow is structured like a dynamic mind map, making complex data aggregation instantly understandable.

Context:
Card 1 visualizes recent activity signals about a person, such as:
- Recent posts
- Achievements
- Engagement signals (likes, reactions, milestones)

These signals are sourced from:
- Website activity
- LinkedIn
- X (Twitter)
- Facebook

The system analyzes these signals and generates a personalized DM.

Mind-Map Flow Requirements:
1) Start with the person/profile node at the center (core of the mind map).
2) Animate branches extending outward to each data source (Website, LinkedIn, X, Facebook).
3) From each source branch, show smaller sub-nodes representing recent activities (posts, achievements, likes).
4) Animate all sub-nodes flowing back toward the center, visually merging into insights.
5) From the center, extend a final branch that resolves into a personalized DM card.

Animation & Design Requirements:
1) The mind-map structure must be obvious at first glance.
2) Use smooth, organic motion (growing lines, connecting nodes, pulsing dots).
3) Ensure the full concept is understandable within 3–5 seconds.
4) Avoid text-heavy explanations; rely on icons, motion, and spatial layout.
5) Clearly communicate cause → effect: activity signals → insight → personalization.
6) The “aha moment” should be when scattered nodes snap together into one clear message.

Style & Constraints:
- Style: Clean, modern, SaaS-friendly, minimalistic.
- Pacing: Fast, but readable; no visual overload.
- Scope: Only Card 1. No extra features or additional cards.
- No new data sources beyond those listed.

Output Format:
- Numbered, step-by-step animation storyboard.
- Each step must include: visual layout, animation behavior, and viewer takeaway.
- Keep instructions precise and implementation-ready for motion designers.

End with a brief self-check confirming the animation feels intuitive, visually engaging, and instantly understandable.
