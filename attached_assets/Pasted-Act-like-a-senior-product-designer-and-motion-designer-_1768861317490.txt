Act like a senior product designer and motion designer specializing in SaaS onboarding animations and personalized outreach experiences.

Your objective is to design a clear, eye-catching, and easily understandable animation concept for “Card 1” in a product flow, where recent activity data is used to personalize a direct message (DM).

Task:
Create a step-by-step animation concept that visually explains how recent activity data is collected from multiple platforms and transformed into a personalized DM.

Context:
Card 1 displays recent activity signals about a person, such as:
- Recent posts
- Achievements
- Engagement signals (likes, comments, milestones)

This data is sourced from multiple platforms, including:
- Website activity
- LinkedIn
- X (Twitter)
- Facebook

Based on this aggregated data, the system generates a personalized DM tailored to that individual.

Requirements:
1) Clearly show data sources (Website, LinkedIn, X, Facebook) as distinct visual elements.
2) Visually represent “recent activity” (posts, achievements, likes) in a simple, non-cluttered way.
3) Animate the transformation from raw activity data into a personalized DM.
4) Ensure the animation is understandable within 3–5 seconds, even without text or narration.
5) Use motion to guide the viewer’s eye and communicate cause → effect (data → personalization).
6) Avoid technical jargon; rely on universally understandable visual metaphors (cards, icons, flow, glow, merge).

Animation Guidelines:
- Style: Modern, clean, SaaS-style motion design.
- Pacing: Fast but readable; no overwhelming transitions.
- Visual hierarchy: Sources → Activity → Personalization → Final DM.
- Emphasis: The “aha moment” when personalization is created.

Output Format:
- Step-by-step animation storyboard (numbered steps).
- Each step should include: visual elements, motion/transition, and user takeaway.
- Keep descriptions concise and implementation-ready.

Constraints:
- Do not design additional cards or features beyond Card 1.
- Do not introduce new data sources beyond those listed.
- Focus only on explaining the scenario visually, not selling or marketing copy.
- Verify clarity: a first-time viewer should understand the concept instantly.

End with a short self-check confirming that the animation is eye-catching, simple, and self-explanatory.

Take a deep breath and work on this problem step-by-step.
